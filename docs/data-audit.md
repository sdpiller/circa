# Data audit

Phase 0 gate report. The findings below are written by hand from the generated tables further down (sections A to I, regenerated by `npm run audit`). Data: Wikidata via WDQS and QLever, fetched 2026-10-28. How the fetch works: `docs/decisions.md`, entries 8 and 15 to 17.

## Summary

- **Accuracy is good.** All four year-convention spot checks pass end to end (section I), and the Greece 500 to 300 BCE top list (section H) has correct years and coordinates for every famous battle checked.
- **The strict event set is about 27,000 usable events, and it is overwhelmingly war.** 79% are conflict (section B). In the owner's focus area, Greece 500 to 300 BCE, 24 of the top 25 events are battles or wars; the only exception is the Plague of Athens. Narratives built on this alone would read like a military timeline.
- **Coverage is thin, sometimes zero, outside the Mediterranean before about 1500.** See "Where coverage is thin" below.
- **Recommended sitelink threshold: none (keep the fetch floor of 1).** Rank by sitelinks, do not filter. Reasons below.
- **Pending:** settlement foundings (partly downloaded), structures, archaeological sites, and births are still downloading. Headline numbers above cover the event classes only and will be redone when everything is in. Their findings go in the "Candidates" section and decide whether the war-heavy problem is fixable.

## Where coverage is thin

Strict events, from sections D and G:

- **Pre-Columbian Americas: effectively nothing.** Zero events before 501 CE in all of the Americas; 3 events from 501 to 1000; Mesoamerica has 4 and the Andes 6 events for 1001 to 1500. Nearly all American events are after 1500, which means colonial and later history.
- **Sub-Saharan Africa: 2 events before 1001 CE**, 14 for 1001 to 1500. West Africa has none before 1001 CE.
- **South Asia** is sparse in antiquity: the Ganges plain has 5 events for 500 to 300 BCE and 2 for 1 to 500 CE.
- **Egypt is surprisingly thin**: 4 events for 500 to 300 BCE and 1 for 1 to 500 CE. Pharaonic history before 1000 BCE barely registers (the whole world has 32 events before 1000 BCE).
- **Eastern China** is moderate: 64 events for 1 to 500 CE but only 4 for 500 to 300 BCE.
- **Dense:** Europe holds 45% of all events. Greece and Italy are the best-covered ancient areas by far (146 and 91 events for 500 to 300 BCE).

This is a property of what Wikidata models as dated, located events, not of history. The required caption ("documented history is not the same as history") is necessary but will not make empty Mesoamerica feel less empty.

## Location confidence

58% of strict events have their own coordinates (exact), 41% inherit from a P276 place, 1% from P131 (section C). Only 3% had several candidate places to choose from. Wars, treaties, revolutions, and coups mostly lose their location (48 to 67% of each class rejected, mainly as "no-location" or "country-only", in section A), because they point at countries. That is the intended cost of never using country centroids, and it removes most of the "big picture" items (wars) while keeping their battles.

## Recommended sitelink threshold: none (floor of 1)

- **A threshold above 2 deepens the regional bias.** At ≥5, Europe keeps 46% and the Middle East 51% of their events, but South Asia keeps 36%, Sub-Saharan Africa 38%, and the Americas south of 15°N 34% (section F, region table). At ≥2, every region keeps 75 to 84%, so ≥2 is neutral, but it still costs 21% of events for no benefit.
- **Single-sitelink events are real, specific history.** A sample (2026-10-28) included the Battle of the Himera River (406 BCE), Siege of Uruk (about 2800 BCE), Elean War, Battle of Petilia (71 BCE), and Siege of Themiscyra. For antiquity, they are the long tail narratives need.
- **The database is tiny** (about 27,000 rows), so there is no storage or speed reason to cut. Level of detail already shows only the top N by sitelinks per viewport (decision 3), so clutter is handled by ranking, not filtering.
- **The real cost of low-sitelink events shows up in Phase 5:** only 37% of single-sitelink events have an English Wikipedia article (88% at ≥2). Without an article there is no extract to ground the narrative, only a label and a one-line description. Phase 5 should prefer events with an article for the narrative and treat label-only events as "mentioned, not described".

Keep the threshold configurable (`minSitelinks` per class in `etl/classes.json`) so this can be revisited.

## Other findings to act on in Phase 1

- **Label fallback: done, small gain, no bias effect.** The first audit pass rejected 3,093 events for having no English label (massacres 755, earthquakes 637, treaties 498), and I guessed these were mostly non-European events. **That guess was wrong.** After fetching labels in every language for just those items, the fallback (`chooseLabel`: English, then `mul`, then a preference list) recovers about 1,800 usable events, about 6.5% more strict events; the rest also lacked a date or location. Their labels are mostly French (716), Russian (446), Spanish (159), and German (98). Every region relies on non-English labels for only 1 to 5% of its events, except the Americas south of 15°N at 9%, mostly Spanish (section C2). `mul` labels barely occur. Conclusion: keep the fallback (cheap, recovers real events, helps Latin America most), but the coverage bias comes from what Wikidata models, not from labels. The UI and narrative should know when a label is not English (`labelLang` on each record).
- **Dedupe:** 925 events matched more than one class (for example both battle and siege); they are resolved by category priority.
- **Multi-valued dates** are common for famous events (Marathon has three P585 values with different ranks). Rank handling works as designed.
