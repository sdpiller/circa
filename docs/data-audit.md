# Data audit

Phase 0 gate report. The findings below are written by hand from the generated tables further down (sections A to I, regenerated by `npm run audit`). Data: Wikidata via WDQS and QLever, fetched 2026-10-28 (884 queries). How the fetch works: `docs/decisions.md`, entries 8 and 15 to 17.

Four groups are reported separately throughout:

| Group | Usable items | What it is |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Events | 28,650 | Battles, sieges, treaties, earthquakes, and the other event classes in the plan |
| Settlement foundings | 73,345 | Settlements with an inception date (in the plan, as the "founding" category) |
| Structures and sites | 53,670 | Built structures (≥5 sitelinks) and archaeological sites with an inception date. Candidate, not in the plan |
| Births | 85,013 | People with a birth date and a located birthplace (≥20 sitelinks). Candidate, not in the plan |

## Summary

- **Accuracy is good.** All four year-convention spot checks pass end to end (section I). Years and coordinates in the Greece top lists (section H) are right for every famous item checked.
- **Events alone are overwhelmingly war, and worst in antiquity.** 76% of all events are conflict (section B), and from the 5th century BCE to the 10th century CE it is 94 to 100% per century (section E). In Greece, 500 to 300 BCE, 24 of the top 25 events are battles or wars.
- **The candidate types fix that for the Mediterranean.** For Greece 500 to 300 BCE they add the Platonic Academy (387 BCE), the Lyceum, the Mausoleum at Halicarnassus, the Statue of Zeus at Olympia, and the Athena Parthenos, plus the births of Aristotle, Plato, Socrates, Sophocles, Herodotus, and Hippocrates (section H).
- **They do not fix the regional gaps.** Mesoamerica, the Andes, and West Africa stay at or near zero in every group before 500 CE (section G).
- **Recommended sitelink threshold: none (keep the floor of 1).** Rank by sitelinks; do not filter. Reasons below.
- **Decision needed:** whether structures and births enter the product. Recommendation at the end.

## Where coverage is thin

Events, from sections D and G (the other groups barely change this):

- **Pre-Columbian Americas: effectively nothing.** Zero events before 501 CE anywhere in the Americas, 3 from 501 to 1000. Mesoamerica has 4 events and the Andes 6 for 1001 to 1500. American history in the data is colonial and later.
- **Sub-Saharan Africa: 2 events before 1001 CE**, 14 for 1001 to 1500. West Africa has nothing in any group before 1 CE.
- **South Asia** is sparse in antiquity: the Ganges plain has 5 events for 500 to 300 BCE and 2 for 1 to 500 CE.
- **Egypt is surprisingly thin**: 4 events for 500 to 300 BCE and 1 for 1 to 500 CE. The whole world has 32 events before 1000 BCE.
- **Eastern China** is moderate: 65 events for 1 to 500 CE but only 4 for 500 to 300 BCE.
- **Dense:** Europe holds 45% of all events. Greece and Italy are by far the best-covered ancient areas (146 and 91 events for 500 to 300 BCE).

This reflects what Wikidata models as dated, located items, not history. The required caption ("documented history is not the same as history") is necessary but will not make empty Mesoamerica feel less empty. No source on the current list fills those gaps: the Backlog's Pleiades gazetteer covers the ancient Mediterranean only, which is already the best-covered area.

## Location confidence

57% of events have their own coordinates (exact), 41% inherit from a P276 place, 1% from P131 (section C). Settlement foundings and structures are almost all exact. Births are 100% inherited by construction: the location is the birthplace, which is often not where the person mattered (Aristotle was born in Stagira and worked in Athens).

Wars, treaties, revolutions, and coups mostly lose their location (48 to 67% of each class rejected, mainly as "no-location" or "country-only", in section A), because they point at countries. That is the intended cost of never using country centroids: the "big picture" wars drop out while their battles stay.

## Recommended sitelink threshold: none (floor of 1)

- **Any threshold deepens the regional bias.** At ≥2, most regions keep 73 to 81% of their events, but the Americas south of 15°N keep only 66%. At ≥5, Europe keeps 43% and the Middle East 48%, but South Asia keeps 35%, Sub-Saharan Africa 37%, and the Americas south of 15°N 29% (section F, region table).
- **Single-sitelink events are real, specific history.** A sample included the Battle of the Himera River (406 BCE), the Siege of Uruk (about 2800 BCE), the Elean War, the Battle of Petilia (71 BCE), and the Siege of Themiscyra. For antiquity they are the long tail narratives need.
- **The data is small** (about 240,000 items across all groups), so there is no storage or speed reason to cut. Level of detail shows only the top N per viewport (decision 3), so clutter is handled by ranking, not filtering.
- **The real cost of low-sitelink events shows up in Phase 5:** only 37% of single-sitelink events have an English Wikipedia article (88% at ≥2). Without an article there is no extract to ground the narrative, only a label and a one-line description. Phase 5 should prefer items with an article and treat label-only items as "mentioned, not described".

The candidate groups were fetched with floors (structures ≥5, births ≥20) to keep the fetch polite. Those floors are fetch limits, not recommendations. Keep `minSitelinks` per class in `etl/classes.json` so this can be revisited.

## Findings to act on in Phase 1

- **Rank within each group, never across groups.** Births have far more sitelinks than events (median 27 versus 3; Aristotle 319 versus Thermopylae 83, section F). One mixed ranking would fill the globe with birthplaces. The events API needs a quota per group per viewport.
- **Cap absurdly wide intervals.** A few items have precision 4 or coarser, for example the Dispilio Neolithic Settlement at "100000 BCE to 1 BCE". Under the overlap rule they match every range query. Proposal: skip items whose effective interval is wider than 1,000 years.
- **Settlement foundings are mostly modern and mostly Europe and North America** (Europe 39,930, Americas north of 15°N 14,505; about 43,000 in the 19th and 20th centuries). Fine with per-group ranking, but they would swamp modern ranges otherwise.
- **"Inception" is not always "built".** The Temple of Artemis at Ephesus shows 323 BCE, the date of the rebuilt temple. UI copy should say "dated" or "inception", not "built", unless the narrative's source says so.
- **Label fallback: done, modest gain.** The first pass rejected 3,093 events for having no English label (massacres 755, earthquakes 637, treaties 498), and I guessed these were mostly non-European events. **That guess was wrong.** Falling back to other languages (`chooseLabel`: English, then `mul`, then a preference list) recovered about 1,800 events, labelled mostly in French (700), Russian (445), Spanish (148), and German (71). Regions rely on non-English labels for 2 to 10% of their events, except the Americas south of 15°N at 15% (section C2). Keep the fallback, and record the label language (`labelLang`) so the UI and narrative know when a label is not English.
- **Dedupe:** 3,008 items matched more than one class; they are resolved by category priority.
- **Multi-valued dates** are common for famous events (Marathon has three P585 values with different ranks). Rank handling works as designed.

## Year-zero findings

Full detail in `docs/decisions.md`, entries 10 and 11, with tests in `tests/time.test.ts`. In short: entity JSON uses historical numbering (Marathon is `-0490`), while SPARQL uses astronomical numbering (`-0489`) and shifts day-precision Julian dates to Gregorian. Year-precision dates are not shifted. Decades floor and centuries ceil, matching Wikibase's own formatter. All four required events come out right (section I).

## Decision needed at the gate

**Do structures and sites, and births, enter the product?**

1. **Events and settlement foundings only (the plan as written).** Simplest. Ancient narratives will be military timelines, because that is what the data holds.
2. **Add structures and sites. Recommended, low risk.** 98% exact locations, mostly year-precision dates, and they carry the culture of antiquity (temples, theatres, the Academy). Costs: one more group in the schema and in the API quota.
3. **Also add births. Recommended, with conditions.** They are the biggest single improvement for narratives ("Socrates was born in Athens around 470 BCE"), and Wikipedia extracts can say what the person did. Conditions: rank and cap them separately; show them as people, not events (a `kind` column: event, founding, structure, birth, instead of stretching the `culture` category); and accept that the location is a birthplace (hollow dot, "born here").

My recommendation is **2 and 3**, which means a schema change in Phase 1 (add `kind`, logged in `docs/decisions.md`). If you prefer a smaller v1, 2 alone is still a clear improvement over the plan.
