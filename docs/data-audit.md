# Data audit

Phase 0 gate report. The findings below are written by hand from the generated tables further down (sections A to I, regenerated by `npm run audit`). Data: Wikidata via WDQS and QLever, fetched 2026-10-28. How the fetch works: `docs/decisions.md`, entries 8 and 15 to 17. Gate decisions: entries 18 and 19.

Five kinds are reported separately throughout:

| Kind | Usable items | What it is |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Events | 40,463 | Battles, treaties, earthquakes, protests, accidents, councils, and the other event classes (`etl/classes.json`) |
| Settlement foundings | 73,344 | Settlements with an inception date |
| Structures and sites | 53,562 | Built structures (≥5 sitelinks) and archaeological sites with an inception date |
| Births | 85,011 | People with a birth date and a located birthplace (≥20 sitelinks) |
| Discoveries | 3,095 | Items with a time of discovery, placed at the find spot |

## Summary

- **Accuracy is good.** All four year-convention spot checks pass end to end (section I). Years and coordinates in the Greece top lists (section H) are right for every famous item checked.
- **Events in antiquity are almost all war.** 60% of all events are conflict (section B), but from the 5th to the 1st century BCE it is 94 to 100% per century, and still 87 to 93% in the 1st, 5th, and 10th centuries CE (section E). In Greece, 500 to 300 BCE, 24 of the top 25 events are battles or wars. The non-war event classes added at the gate (protests, accidents, councils, and more) mostly enrich the modern era.
- **Structures and births fix that for the Mediterranean** (approved at the gate). For Greece 500 to 300 BCE they add the Platonic Academy (387 BCE), the Lyceum, the Mausoleum at Halicarnassus, the Statue of Zeus at Olympia, and the Athena Parthenos, plus the births of Aristotle, Plato, Socrates, Sophocles, Herodotus, and Hippocrates (section H).
- **Discoveries add located science and archaeology**, dated by when they were found: the electron (Cambridge, 1897), elements in their labs, Lucy (Hadar, 1974), the Rosetta Stone (Rashid, 1799), the tomb of Tutankhamun (1922), the Terracotta Army (1974). They add nothing to ancient eras on the range slider, because the date is the discovery.
- **Nothing fixes the regional gaps.** Mesoamerica, the Andes, and West Africa stay at or near zero in every kind before 500 CE (section G).
- **Recommended sitelink threshold: none (keep the floor of 1).** Rank by sitelinks; do not filter. Reasons below.

## Where coverage is thin

Events, from sections D and G (the other kinds barely change this):

- **Pre-Columbian Americas: effectively nothing.** Zero events before 501 CE anywhere in the Americas, 3 from 501 to 1000. Mesoamerica has 4 events and the Andes 6 for 1001 to 1500. American history in the data is colonial and later.
- **Sub-Saharan Africa: 2 events before 1001 CE**, 16 for 1001 to 1500. West Africa has nothing in any kind before 1 CE.
- **South Asia** is sparse in antiquity: the Ganges plain has 5 events for 500 to 300 BCE and 2 for 1 to 500 CE.
- **Egypt is surprisingly thin**: 4 events for 500 to 300 BCE and 8 for 1 to 500 CE. The whole world has 33 events before 1000 BCE.
- **Eastern China** is moderate: 65 events for 1 to 500 CE but only 4 for 500 to 300 BCE.
- **Dense:** Europe holds 44% of all events. Greece and Italy are by far the best-covered ancient areas (148 and 93 events for 500 to 300 BCE).

This reflects what Wikidata models as dated, located items, not history. The required caption ("documented history is not the same as history") is necessary but will not make empty Mesoamerica feel less empty. No source on the current list fills those gaps: the Backlog's Pleiades gazetteer covers the ancient Mediterranean only, which is already the best-covered area.

## Location confidence

58% of events have their own coordinates (exact), 41% inherit from a P276 place, 2% from P131 (section C). Settlement foundings and structures are almost all exact. Births are 100% inherited by construction: the location is the birthplace, which is often not where the person mattered (Aristotle was born in Stagira and worked in Athens). Discoveries are placed at the find spot (P189) for 60% of items and at their own coordinates for 40%.

Wars, treaties, revolutions, and coups mostly lose their location (48 to 67% of each class rejected, mainly as "no-location" or "country-only", in section A), because they point at countries. That is the intended cost of never using country centroids: the "big picture" wars drop out while their battles stay.

## Recommended sitelink threshold: none (floor of 1)

- **Any threshold cuts the long tail everywhere, and hardest in Latin America.** At ≥5, regions keep only 31 to 46% of their events: the Americas south of 15°N keep 31%, South Asia 36%, Europe 38%, Sub-Saharan Africa 39%, the Middle East 46% (section F, region table). An earlier pass, before the extra classes, showed a sharper Europe-versus-rest gap; with the new low-sitelink European events that gap mostly closed, so bias is no longer the main argument.
- **Single-sitelink events are real, specific history.** A sample included the Battle of the Himera River (406 BCE), the Siege of Uruk (about 2800 BCE), the Elean War, the Battle of Petilia (71 BCE), and the Siege of Themiscyra. For antiquity they are the long tail narratives need.
- **The data is small** (about 255,000 items across all kinds), so there is no storage or speed reason to cut. Level of detail shows only the top N per viewport (decision 3), so clutter is handled by ranking, not filtering.
- **The real cost of low-sitelink events shows up in Phase 5:** only 37% of single-sitelink events have an English Wikipedia article (88% at ≥2, measured on the first pass). Without an article there is no extract to ground the narrative, only a label and a one-line description. Phase 5 should prefer items with an article and treat label-only items as "mentioned, not described".

Structures and births were fetched with floors (≥5 and ≥20 sitelinks) to keep the fetch polite. Those floors are fetch limits, not recommendations. Keep `minSitelinks` per class in `etl/classes.json` so this can be revisited.

## Findings to act on in Phase 1

- **Rank within each kind, never across kinds.** Births have far more sitelinks than events (median 27 versus 3; Aristotle 319 versus Thermopylae 83, section F). One mixed ranking would fill the globe with birthplaces. The events API needs a quota per kind per viewport.
- **Cap absurdly wide intervals: done in Phase 1.** Items whose effective interval is wider than 1,000 years match nearly every range query under the overlap rule, so they are dropped (`MAX_INTERVAL_YEARS` in `etl/build.ts`). Only 10 items, all genuinely vague: "neolithic revolution" (a 7,000-year span), births at ten-millennia precision, and caves and sites dated "100000 BCE to 1 BCE" such as the Dispilio Neolithic Settlement. The first version of the cap also caught Kyoto and Stonehenge; see the next point.
- **Settlement foundings are mostly modern and mostly Europe and North America** (Europe 39,930, Americas north of 15°N 14,505; about 43,000 in the 19th and 20th centuries). Fine with per-kind ranking, but they would swamp modern ranges otherwise.
- **"Inception" is not always "built".** The Temple of Artemis at Ephesus shows 323 BCE, the date of the rebuilt temple. UI copy should say "dated" or "inception", not "built", unless the narrative's source says so.
- **Label fallback: done, modest gain.** The first pass rejected 3,093 events for having no English label, and I guessed these were mostly non-European events. **That guess was wrong.** Falling back to other languages (`chooseLabel`: English, then `mul`, then a preference list) recovers them; across 40,463 events, 3,313 rely on a non-English label, mostly French (906), Russian (708), German (320), and Spanish (260). Per region it ranges from 2% to 17%, highest in Russia and Central Asia (17%) and the Americas south of 15°N (14%), with Europe at 10% (section C2). Keep the fallback, and record the label language (`labelLang`) so the UI and narrative know when a label is not English.
- **Several values: most precise, then earliest.** When several equal-rank values of one date property disagree, only the most precise are kept, and of those the earliest wins. Spanning them was wrong in both directions: Lascaux (discovered 1940, plus a misfiled millennium-precision value for the paintings' age) became a 19,000-year interval, and Kyoto (inceptions 794 for Heian-kyo and 1889 for the modern municipality) a 1,100-year "founding" that the width cap then dropped, as it did Stonehenge. Real spans come only from explicit start and end properties (P580 and P582).
- **Dedupe:** 9,956 items matched more than one class; they are resolved by kind priority, then category priority.
- **Multi-valued dates** are common for famous events (Marathon has three P585 values with different ranks). Rank handling works as designed.

## Discoveries: placement rules (added at the gate)

Discoveries are defined by properties, not a class: a time of discovery (P575) plus either a find spot (P189) or the item's own coordinates. Their location must never be P276, which on museum objects is the museum (the Rosetta Stone's P276 is the British Museum). Own coordinates are trusted only for items outside museum collections that are archaeological sites, built structures, caves, meteorites, or impact craters. A blocklist of geographic classes was tried first and kept leaking centroids with European "discovery" dates (Brazil 1500, Greenland, Norfolk Island, Bora Bora, the Gulf Stream); the allowlist removed all of them while keeping Lascaux, Chauvet, Persepolis, Borobudur, Abu Simbel, and the tomb of Tutankhamun (section H2).

## Year-zero findings

Full detail in `docs/decisions.md`, entries 10 and 11, with tests in `tests/time.test.ts`. In short: entity JSON uses historical numbering (Marathon is `-0490`), while SPARQL uses astronomical numbering (`-0489`) and shifts day-precision Julian dates to Gregorian. Year-precision dates are not shifted. Decades floor and centuries ceil, matching Wikibase's own formatter. All four required events come out right (section I).

## Gate decision (made 2026-10-28)

The owner chose to add structures and sites, and births (options 2 and 3), as separate kinds; then, after a gap scan, civil and social events, accidents and disasters, more conflict classes, and discoveries. Sports and entertainment editions are excluded. Phase 1 adds a `kind` column to the schema.
